# Hard questions: Jarvis (AGENTHON)
| # | Question they will ask | Short answer (facts) | Fact IDs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two sources disagree. Who wins? | Neither, automatically. For example, two prices for the same plan: Jarvis shows both side by side, and the owner decides: replace, reject or edit. The old version stays in history. | F05, F12 |
| 2 | What stops a confident but wrong answer with a real-looking source? | Only ids we gave the model count as citations, and every number must appear in the cited facts. Otherwise we show the approved fact word for word, or say "I don't know". | F05 |
| 3 | Can someone talk it into showing finance data? | No. The role filter runs in code before the model sees anything: the finance group and CEO can read the finance fact, Andrea (CTO) is refused. Typing "I'm the CFO" changes nothing. | F05, F13 |
| 4 | Can the agent send an email or approve something by itself? | No. There is no send tool. Drafts wait for the named person's tap. On the phone it refuses "approve it". | F05, F10 |
| 5 | What about prompt injection or fraud emails? | Patterns catch the common and disguised ones, and a second Gemini check caught a reworded attack the patterns missed. Suspicious emails are flagged and don't become memory by themselves. | F08, F09 |
| 6 | Is this really agentic, or chat over documents? | It runs on its own: it briefs on a schedule while the app is open (a real deployment needs a server job and live connectors), reads new email, flags an urgent one and alerts the CEO (an in-app call card; a real phone call when configured and opted in), and drafts messages and Zoom meetings that wait for approval. Each step is logged. | F03, F10, F12 |
| 7 | What does it cost? | About $0.003 per morning brief: one live 08:00 sweep for 7 people cost about $0.022 on this build (Gemini 3.8 Flash, 21 Nov). The cost of a phone call isn't measured yet. | F24 |
| 8 | What if Gemini or the Wi-Fi fails on stage? | If Gemini fails, nothing is sent without a person's tap, the role filter still runs in code, and the screen shows it is offline. The second AI injection check is off then; only the pattern layer runs. I'll show you. | F05 |
| 9 | GDPR? Where does the data go? | Demo data only today (synthetic). The AI runs on Google Vertex AI. We don't yet claim what reaches the voice provider: that needs its own verified fix. A real deployment needs a data processing agreement (DPA) and a company login. | F13 |
| 10 | Who would pay, and how big is the market? | Small firms with scattered knowledge, starting with Ariete's clients through a pilot. We haven't sized the market yet. | F14 |
| 11 | Is it production-ready? | No. Identity is a picker, the connectors are demo copies (a phone call and Zoom work only with keys and opt-in; Stripe is off in the Ariete demo), and there's no persistence beyond files. That is the pilot's job. | F12 |
| 12 | Why three builds? | We built three versions in parallel, attacked them with our own red-team agents, and merged the best ideas. All three passed the 7 checks. | F07, F17 |
| 13 | How is this different from Copilot or Glean? | I won't speak for their products. Here's what you saw live: memory holds only what a person approved. The role filter runs in code before the model sees anything. And it alerts you, but never acts without a tap. | F05 |
| 14 | Can anyone just pick "CFO" in the picker? | In the demo, yes: the picker stands in for a company login (named as a limit). The rules after login are in code. | F05 |
| 15 | Does data leave the EU? | The AI runs on Google Vertex AI on the global location today; an EU region is a setting for a real deployment. | F13 |
| 16 | Does it work in Italian? | The model reads Italian; our demo data is mostly English. Not tested in Italian yet. | — |
| 17 | When does it decide to call? | Only when the model says the email is urgent AND the code finds urgency words; never for a suspicious email; once per email. | F10, F05 |
| 18 | What would the pilot with Ariete look like? | One client firm, read-only copies of their tools, a few weeks, and a person approving every fact and action. Terms to agree together. | F14 |
| 19 | Who has time to approve everything? | Each area has one owner (finance by the CFO), and volume is the pilot's open question: the next step is batching low-risk facts. | F05 |
| 20 | Does Jarvis know about the company itself? | Only what the CEO approved: the company profile, the team and how Ariete works with clients (mem-020, mem-021, mem-022, citing the client handbook). Anything else gets "I don't know", by design. | F05 |
| 21 | Who pays? | Ariete: it resells Jarvis inside its AI-agent service to its client firms. The price is set together in the pilot; we won't guess a number today. | F14 |
| 22 | What would the pilot measure? | Urgent emails caught before anyone missed them, questions answered with a source vs "I don't know", approvals per person per day, attacks blocked. The audit log already records every one of these. | F05 |
| 23 | Your AI runs on Google's global location, but Ariete sells EU data centres. | True today. In the pilot it runs in an EU region, or with an open-weight model on the client's own servers, which Ariete already offers. | F22 |
